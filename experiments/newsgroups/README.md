# The linguistic field

A control on the mixing-law claim, not evidence for it. Split out of
`rate-fields`, which now fits and draws the two physical fields only.

## Why it is separate

The two physical fields are instruments that count: a photon counter and a Geiger tube, where
Section 2 is the measurement process rather than an analogy. Both are fitted at a negative
order, outside the range a gamma mixing law can reach however its two parameters are set.

This field counts tokens, so the process has the same shape, but nothing physical is being
measured. It is here to answer a different question — whether the argument for a third
parameter is about counting or about the particular fields chosen to make it — and it answers
it by disagreeing:

| field | n | ΔAIC gamma | ΔAIC lognormal | fitted order | per observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 Newsgroups terms | 22460 | 7 | 1362 | **+1.73** | 0.00 |

The order comes out **positive**, inside the gamma's own range, and the third parameter is
worth 7 AIC points over 22460 observations — 0.0003 each. The gamma and the generalised
inverse Gaussian coincide, which is why the figure draws the gamma as a halo underneath
rather than as a line of its own.

Reported beside the two physical fields this reads as one result over three fields, which is
the opposite of what it is. It earns its place by being the case that does not agree.

## What it shares with the physical fields

The laws, the fitting loop and the row format are imported from `rate-fields`
(`compare.fit_fields`, `compare.write_rows`, `fields.LINGUISTIC_FIELDS`), so the two cannot
drift apart. The figure matches the physical panels in palette, axes and annotation, and its
width is half the two-panel figure so a panel is the same size on the page either way.

## One caveat on the null result

The field excludes terms absent from any group: an observed zero there stands in for an
unknown positive rate, and fitting it as a zero would measure the truncation rather than the
field. That removes the rare bursty terms where a heavy tail would show most, so the exclusion
works *against* the null result here rather than for it.

## Running

```bash
cd experiments/newsgroups
python run.py          # writes results/newsgroup_fits.csv
python visualize.py    # writes figures/newsgroups.pdf
```

The table is generated by `experiments/make_tables.py` into
`experiments/tables/newsgroups.tex`, which is **not** yet `\input` anywhere in `main.tex`.

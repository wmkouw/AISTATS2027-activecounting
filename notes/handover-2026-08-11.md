## What is established, in order of how well it holds up

1. **The model result on real data (§5.4).** Three measured rate fields, three mixing laws,
   AIC. The third parameter is decisive on both physical fields and worthless on the
   linguistic one, and the fitted order predicts which: negative where the gamma cannot
   reach, positive where it can. Depends on no acquisition, budget or policy. The strongest
   thing in the paper.
2. **Posterior consistency (Proposition 2, Appendix B).** Proved from an exact bracket on the
   Bessel ratio. The constant in the rate is verified numerically but only sketched.
3. **The scarcity sweep (§5.2).** Adaptive allocation earns its keep only when the budget
   cannot cover the contexts. The transition is sharp, between 10 and 5 m^3 per block. This
   reframed the whole experimental section.
4. **Correct specification and convergence (§5.1).** Solid, verified to machine precision.
5. **The policy comparisons (§5.2, §5.3).** Two tiers: criteria that ignore the aleatoric
   term fail badly, everything that spreads is equivalent. EPIG and D-optimality tie our
   criterion in *every* setting run, at 1/8 and 1/27 of the cost. Our acquisition has never
   separated from them.

## What to be careful about

- **Do not quote §5.2's numbers as regime-free.** They are one point of the sweep, and an
  abundant one. See `.claude/memory/budget-scarcity-decides-whether-acquisition-matters.md`.
- **The claim is not "more accurate than other active sensors."** That is not supported. What
  is supported: the model earns its third parameter on real physical rate fields; the closed
  forms make the acquisition an order of magnitude cheaper than the alternatives that match
  it; and the acquisition matters enormously against criteria that ignore the aleatoric term
  and not at all against ones that merely spread.
- **Overdispersion has two readings** and only one is implemented. See the memory of that
  name. Never settled, and it decides what the baselines should be.
